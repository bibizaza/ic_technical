## 0. Bottom line

1. **The gap has two big parts, and only one is reachable.** In the three large V rebounds (2009, 2020, 2025)
   the first five trading days after the low are 34–59% of the gap. No rule gets them (VGAP: real and failed
   lows look the same until day 21). The second part is **size**: once a re-entry layer is on, it holds
   36–68% of the money on average. Size is 23–45% of the gap.
2. **The keys are not the problem in a shock V.** After S or B fires, the core holds exactly what the keys
   would hold (the volatility size). Month lock, revision lag and next-day trading together are under 6% of
   the gap in every episode.
3. **HTEI: no.** The composite says "cheap and scared", not "the fall is over". It was in the BUY zone 3 to
   50 trading days before every low, with a further fall of 6% to 31%. No single component fixes this with
   data we hold; the VIX curve is the only one with the right meaning, and it adds nothing to S and B.
4. **Honest ceiling.** A risk-budget decision (S and B at full weight) would lift capture in shock Vs from
   about one third to about one half. The cost is a loss of 10–18 points when a re-entry fails; our 1999–2026
   sample contains no failed S or B episode, so that cost is invisible in the back-test. Beyond that, the gap
   cannot be closed.

## 2. The reasons, one by one

### Unavoidable

| Reason | Numbers |
|---|---|
| **Week one.** The first 5 days hold a third to a half of the gap. | S&P in days 1–5: 2009 +11.5%, 2020 +17.5%, 2025 +8.3% (9 April alone +9.5%). VGAP: failed lows gain +5.7% in the same 5 days against +7.3% for real Vs. |
| **Any confirmation gives up the confirmed move.** S waits for +10% off the low. | 2020: 2 days. 2025: 11 days. 1998: 16 days. |
| **Bear rallies look the same.** The mechanisms that miss the V kept the core out of every 2001–2008 rally. | Section 3. |

### Structural, and how much each costs

| Mechanism | What happens | Cost in the Vs | Fixable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer size = 15% / 21-day volatility** | At the low the size is 18% (2020), 39% (2009), 49% (2025). The 21-day window keeps the crash days for a month. The rebound's own up-days count as risk: 9 April 2025 (+9.5%) cut the size from 49% to 32%. | 23–36% of the gap. S&P while the layer was on: +17.9% (2009), +17.6% (2020), +8.2% (2025); core earned about 7.0, 6.5 and 3.9 points of it. | Yes, as a risk-budget choice (idea 1). |
| **Size after the keys come back** | Volatility is still high in the second leg. | 2009: 15% of the gap (avg size 82%). 2020: 9% (77%). 2025: 0%. | Not by another estimator: VGAP 3d found the same Sharpe for 10-day, HAR, blended and VIX sizing. |
| **Keys need the trend to recover** | DMAS ≥ 45 came 23–44 days after the low. Revisions turned 57–90 days after, or not at all in the window. Cash needs both keys negative, so the trend key always opened first; the revision key never drove a V re-entry. | In shock Vs: zero extra, because the layer already holds the same size. In broken-trend lows: 35–81% of the gap. | Only with a trigger that fires in those lows (ideas 2, 3). |
| **Month lock** | Keys back 3–19 days after DMAS ≥ 45 (2009: 19; 2025: 16; 2020: 6). | At most 6% of the gap (2016); negative in 2011 and 2022-10, where the lock helped. Without the lock the full Sharpe is 0.53. X (faster re-entry) failed. | No. Leave it. |
| **Trade the next day (signal t, earn t+2)** | One day of return is skipped at each entry. | Mean over the 13 F/S/B entries: +0.08%. One outlier: 26 March 2020, +6.25% missed at a 16% weight = 1 point. | No. |
| **5-point rounding, 10-point band** | | 1–2% of the gap. | No. |
| **S threshold (+10%)** | 9 April 2025 closed +9.5% off the low; S fired 11 days later. | Zero: the S&P was flat from 9 to 24 April (5,457 → 5,485). | No. Do not tune. |
| **Breadth just missed** | Highest Deemer ratio in the 40 days after the low: 1.90 (2016-02), 1.91 (2022-10), 1.76 (2025), 1.58 (2020), against 1.97. | These lows had no B. | Maybe (idea 2). Do not move 1.97. |

2023 is not a V problem. The low of October 2023 was captured at 95% (the keys stayed invested). The 2023
year gap (+17% vs +26%) comes from two short cash spells in a rising market: January (covered by B from
12 January) and 3 March–5 April (SVB), with DMAS below 45 at the month start and revisions negative until August.

Whole-period perspective (1999-02 → 2026-11, arithmetic, per year): on days when the keys were in cash,
holding less than 100% **added** 0.7 points a year, all missed rebounds included. On days when the keys
were invested, the volatility size **cost** 0.9 points a year (average size 93%; below 100% on 31% of days).
The rebounds are painful to watch but the cash spells pay for themselves; the return given up is the price
of the volatility size, which bought the worst fall of −18.5% instead of −28% (RESULTS_core, rows 1 and 2).

## 4. HTEI

### Why the composite failed as a trigger

|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| 2011-10-03 | 43 days before | −12.3% | day 3 | +6.0% |

| 2025-04-08 | 26 days before | −14.8% | day 3 | +7.6% |
| 2022-10-12, 2023-10-27 | never (score 41 and 46 at the low) | | | |

1. **It is a level, not a turn.** Low score = high fear, cheap, tight conditions. That is true for weeks
   before a low. The rule re-armed every day, so it bought the fall again and again: 28 episodes, 15 stopped.
2. **Six of seven components measure the same state** (volatility level, valuation, financial conditions,
   earnings-yield change, sentiment, positioning). Equal-weighting them makes a better "scared" gauge, not a
   timing signal.
3. **It is silent at the lows without panic** (October 2022, October 2023) — the lows where the core has no trigger.
4. **Where it was right, S, B or F entered 2–13 days later anyway** (2018-12-24, 2020-03-23, 2025-04-08).
5. It is fitted on 2010–2026 and the test lost most in 2018–2026 (Sharpe 0.66 vs 0.86).

## 5. Is the volatility size the biggest drag?

It is the biggest **reachable** drag, and second overall (week one is first).

Information only, in-sample, 1999-02 → 2026-11-13, same execution as the core:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|

| No volatility size anywhere, layers at 100% | 0.71 | 11.2% | −28.2% | +40.9% | +34.1% | +12.7% | 0.48 / 0.94 |
| For scale: S&P from day 6 after the low | | | | +51.0% | +34.4% | +15.3% | |

How to read it:
- Full-weight layers take the three rebounds from about 32% to about 51% of the S&P. In 2020 that is nearly
  all that was available after week one.
- **The unchanged worst fall is an artefact**: there was no failed S or B episode in this window. The evidence
  at full weight elsewhere: Europe S, 8 episodes, one stop at −10.5%; US B on the plain trend rule 1965+,
  10 episodes, none stopped. The stop sits 9–10% below entry for S and 9–18% for B, so one failed episode
  costs about 10–18 points at full weight against 2–7 at the live size.
- Inside an episode at full weight the worst fall was −8.5% (1998), −5.9% (2020), −5.4% (2009), with
  realised volatility of 23–33%.
- Removing the size everywhere is a different product (−28% worst fall). Not proposed.

## 6. Candidates for the next pre-registered tests (ranked)

### 1. L — layers at the weight they were tested at (risk budget)

- **Reason.** S passed in Europe and B passed in the US 1965+ at 100% weight on the plain trend rule. The
  product then runs them at 15%/volatility. Inside an episode the price stop already bounds the loss, so the
  volatility size counts the same risk twice, and it treats the rebound's up-days as danger.
- **Rule.** While an S or B episode is on, its weight = 100% (F stays a 50% floor). Triggers, stops, 126-day
  limit, 5-point rounding and next-day trading unchanged. Pre-declared fallback: max(live size, 50%).
- **Data.** Price only. Fresh sample: six non-European indices not yet opened in this project —
  `N225.INDX` (1965), `TWII.INDX` (1970), `GSPTSE.INDX` (1979), `HSI.INDX` (1982), `KS11.INDX` (1983),
  `AXJO.INDX` (1992). Base in each = plain trend rule (`gauge.dmas_on`, 45/30, month lock) × volatility size
  (`run_fast.v2_from_states`); episodes from `run_s.shock_episodes`; price returns, cash 0%, 10 bp.
  B has no fresh sample (NYSE breadth only): it follows S by symmetry, and this must be said.
- **Helps** 2009, 2020, 2025, 2019. **Hurts** any failed re-entry (Europe 2002 type); none in the US product since 1998.
- **Overfitting.** The idea comes from this in-sample split and the +0.05 Sharpe is overstated (no failure in
  sample). The fresh markets fix that only partly: 1987, 1998, 2020 and 2025 hit all of them together.
- **Gates (one run, S-full vs S-live).** Clusters = entries within 10 trading days across markets.
  - G1. Mean of the six Sharpe differences > 0, and positive in at least 4 of 6.
  - G2. Worst fall no more than 3 points deeper in any market.
  - G3. More than half of the clusters have a positive index return over the episode; also without the
    2020 and 2025 clusters.
  - G4. Worst single episode at full weight no worse than −15%.
  - G5. Placebo (1,000 draws, `default_rng(7)`, `run_s.placebo_spans`): pooled Sharpe gain of S-full over the
    base, p ≤ 0.10.
  - Fewer than 10 clusters = "too few to conclude". G1, G3, G5 pass but G2 or G4 fail → adopt the 50% floor.
- **My odds:** all gates about 1 in 3; G1 alone about 1 in 2. Even on a pass it stays a risk-budget decision
  for the user: it means holding 100% equities at 25–35% volatility for some weeks.

### 2. B20 — Deemer's 20-day breadth thrust

- **Reason.** Breadth is the only information class that has passed here (B: 14 of 14 gates, placebo p 0.001).
  The 10-day rule just missed the lows where the core has no trigger (ratio 1.90 in 2016, 1.91 in 2022).
  Deemer's own second rule is slower and catches broad recoveries that are not explosive.
- **Rule (published, zero free choices).** Thrust day = 20-day total NYSE advances / 20-day total declines
  crosses above 1.72. Same action, size and exits as B. Do not touch 1.97.
- **Data.** Same series as B, 1965-03 → today. I have not computed the 20-day ratio; nobody here has seen its dates.
- **Helps (hoped):** 2016-02, 2022-10, perhaps 2002-10 and 2025. **Could hurt:** more triggers inside 2001–02
  and 2008 rallies. Unknown until run.
- **Overfitting.** Deemer set 1.72 in 2019 after seeing 2016, so 2016 is in-sample for the rule. Europe
  cannot check it. I know which lows B missed, which is why I picked it.
- **Gates.** Spec 6t G1–G8 with **R0+B as the base to beat** (the question is what B20 adds), halves split
  1990, placebo p ≤ 0.10, product check (core + B20 Sharpe ≥ core, worst fall within 2 points), and
  episodes from 2019 (rule public) with total P&L > 0. Fewer than 5 added episodes = "too few to conclude".
- **My odds:** about 1 in 3.

### 3. D — new low with less fear (long shot)

- **Reason.** In a decline of several legs, a new price low on lower volatility means selling without forced
  deleveraging. VSHAPE: 6 US events since 1990 (4 V, 1 failed), which I have seen.
- **Rule.** Keys in cash; the index is 15%+ below its 252-day closing high; the low L is more than 63 days
  after the high (so S cannot fire); the highest 21-day realised volatility since the high is at least 10
  trading days older than L; then a close ≥ 1.10 × L starts an episode. Size and exits as S.
- **Data.** Price only: US 1955–1989 (unseen with this definition) plus the six markets of idea 1 and STOXX 600.
- **Helps:** 2002-10, 2009 (about 7 days before B), 2022-10. **Could hurt:** May 2008 and July 2022 look like
  candidates to me; I have not run it.
- **Gates.** As spec 6s on the pooled sample: Sharpe above the base, placebo p ≤ 0.10, more than half of the
  clusters add value, worst fall within 2 points, at least 10 clusters.
- **My odds:** about 1 in 5, and the payoff in the product is small (lows entered at a 50–65% size, +1 to +3
  points each). Run it only because it reuses the panel built for idea 1.

### Not worth a test

HTEI or any component of it; the VIX curve; a faster or unlocked key; a lower S threshold; another
volatility estimator; same-day execution. Evidence in sections 2, 4 and VGAP 3.

## 7. Plain conclusion

- The core will keep missing the first week of every V. That is 34–59% of the gap and it is the price of not
  buying the seven bear rallies of 2001–2008.
- The second leg can be captured better only by holding more in a confirmed re-entry. That is a choice about
  risk, not a signal. In-sample it moves capture from about 32% to about 51%; out of sample it will be less,
  and a failed episode will cost 10–18 points instead of 2–7.
- Lows inside a broken trend (2002, 2016, 2022) stay missed unless breadth fires. B20 is the one remaining
  idea with a reasonable prior.
- If ideas 1 and 2 fail, stop. The client sentence from VGAP stands: "We do not try to catch the first week;
  we cut the fall and come back in the second leg."

## 8. Caveats

- All new numbers are descriptive and in-sample. The variant table is four looks at one history; it is not a test.
- The split in section 1 depends on the window dates (taken from RESULTS_core and the VSHAPE lows) and on
  the order of the cuts ("nothing on" first, size second).
- Layer episodes: 13 since 1998. Shock Vs: 4. Small numbers everywhere.
- Deemer's 20-day threshold (1.72) is quoted from PAPERS_fable_2026-11-17.md; check his page before pre-registering.
- The six index series of idea 1 were not opened. Their quality (holidays, gaps) is unknown.
