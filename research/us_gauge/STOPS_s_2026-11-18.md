# Shock re-entry stops: what the 1987 failure teaches (information only, 2026-11-18)

All shock (S) episodes at 100% on the plain trend rule: US 1955+, STOXX 600 1994+, and the six indices of spec 6L. All of this data has been seen before; nothing here is a test. Script: scratch `stops.py` (same functions as run_l.py).

## US 1987

- 1-year high 25 Aug 1987. Crash low 19 Oct (−33%). Entry 22 Oct, 3 days after the low, at 21-day volatility 85%.
- 4 Dec 1987: close 0.4% below the 19 Oct low → stop. Index return in the episode −9.8%.
- After the stop there was no further fall: +19% in the next 63 days. It was a retest of the low (a double bottom), not a new crash.
- No re-entry: the S rule needs the low within 63 days of the 1-year high; the 4 Dec low was ~70 days after it. Next S entry: 29 Aug 1988.
- At entry, 1987 looked like 2020: 3 vs 2 days after the low, volatility 85% vs 92%, fall −33% vs −34%.

## Summary

- Stops are small undercuts of the low: median -1.0%.
- After the stop, 10 of 18 fell more than 5% further (e.g. Taiwan 1990 −48%, Japan 1990 −27%, Europe 2002 −19%). The stop protected in about half the cases.
- In the other half the low held (US 1987, Hong Kong 1987 and 1997): 63-day return after the stop, median +6.7%.
- Stopped entries came faster (median 8 vs 16 days after the low) and at higher volatility (39% vs 27%). But COVID 2020, the best winner, was also fast and at high volatility, so this cannot be used as a filter.
